## Emerging Patterns

### 1. The AI Governance Trilemma
Korea is simultaneously racing to lead in AI investment (KRW 5.5T R&D), regulate AI (world's first AI Basic Law), and manage AI's disruptive effects (copyright bans, fair use guidelines, job displacement concerns). These three objectives are in tension: aggressive investment pushes adoption ahead of regulatory capacity, while strict regulation could slow the very competitiveness the investment aims to build. The 24% agentic AI adoption rate suggests the market is already outpacing governance frameworks.

### 2. Energy-Trade Double Bind
Korea faces converging pressures from two directions: Middle East tensions threatening energy supply (oil at $100+, helium shortages) and US trade pressure threatening export demand (Section 301 investigation targeting 84% of manufacturing). The semiconductor sector sits at the intersection — it drives record exports but depends on Middle East helium and faces potential US tariffs. This double bind makes Korea uniquely vulnerable to simultaneous supply-side and demand-side shocks.

### 3. Societal Restructuring Under Demographic Pressure
With a TFR of 0.62, 42% single-person households, and 121 consecutive months of birth decline, Korea is confronting an irreversible demographic transformation. The policy response is finally pivoting from futile pro-natalist spending toward structural adaptation: retirement age extension, labor law reform (Yellow Envelope Law), expanded childcare, and a reconceptualization of social contracts. The June local elections and pending legislation make 2026 a decisive year for whether Korea can build institutional resilience for a shrinking population.
