## Emerging Patterns

### Pattern 1: The Great Regulatory Divergence
A striking divergence in governance philosophy is emerging across domains. The US is pursuing sector-specific AI regulation with sandboxes while the EU delays its centralized AI Act. Crypto regulation is clarifying in the US (commodities classification) while the FATF Travel Rule and MiCA tighten globally. This creates a fragmented regulatory landscape that multinational organizations must navigate with increasing sophistication, but it also creates opportunities for regulatory arbitrage.

### Pattern 2: Geopolitical Risk as the New Normal for Supply Chains
The Iran war is not an isolated shock but the latest in a series of geopolitical disruptions (trade wars, pandemic, chip restrictions) that are permanently reshaping global supply chains. Semiconductor supply, energy pricing, food systems, and trade routes are all simultaneously under stress. The WTO's sharp trade growth downgrade (4.6% to 1.9%) and McKinsey's "geometry of trade" analysis both confirm that the post-Cold War era of frictionless globalization has definitively ended.

### Pattern 3: Technology Acceleration Outpacing Governance and Security
AI, quantum computing, autonomous vehicles, gene editing, and fusion energy are all hitting deployment milestones simultaneously. However, security frameworks (AI agent breaches affecting 1 in 8 companies), regulatory timelines (EU AI Act delays), workforce readiness (only 54% using AI), and ethical governance (NIST still in comment phase) are lagging behind. This gap between capability and control is widening and represents a systemic risk.
